Every one of these 15 trades enters at exactly 09:15 and exits at exactly 09:30 with a "target" label but a loss. The Black-Scholes premium estimate at the opening bar is unreliable — no prior bar exists for volatility calculation. The backtest's "fallback target check" fires on a bad estimate. These are not real trades. The fix is to add a warmup bar skip: don't generate signals in bar 0 of the session (09:15 bar).

Finding 2 — The "SL then target" pattern you described IS happening

The breakeven trap is creating near-entry exits — this is the "stopped then reversed" pattern

346 trades (30% of all trades) exit between -₹100 and +₹50. These are all near-entry exits. Here is exactly what happens in each one:

ENTRY
Trade enters. Original SL set at ~30% below entry premium.
E.g. buy call at ₹200. Original SL = ₹140 (30% below).
STAGE 0
Price moves 0.4R in your favour. Breakeven trap fires.
At ₹200 + 0.4×₹60 = ₹224 → SL moved up to ₹200 + 0.1×₹60 = ₹206. Now SL is above your entry.
REVERSE
Market noise or real reversal brings premium back below ₹206.
You exit at ₹206 — barely above entry. Net PnL: ~₹40. This labels as a "win" but you captured almost nothing.
TARGET
If the move had continued, target was ₹200 + 3×₹60 = ₹380.
You missed ₹174 of potential gain per lot because normal market noise triggered the near-breakeven SL.

This is NOT a "false breakout" in the traditional sense. The original entry signal was correct — the move started. The problem is the breakeven trap's SL gets moved too tight (entry + 10% of risk), making it vulnerable to 1-bar retracements that have nothing to do with trade invalidation.

Finding 3 — Signal flags cannot predict individual trade outcomes

Win and loss trades carry almost identical signal fingerprints

Signal comparison: LiqSweep wins 59% losses 61%, FVG wins 41% losses 42%, VP wins 66% losses 65%, OTE wins 23% losses 21%.
Wins Losses

The signals in winning trades and losing trades are nearly identical. LiqSweep appears in 59% of wins and 61% of losses. This means: you cannot filter out "bad" trades by looking at which signals fired. The signal selects the type of entry (confluence quality), not the individual outcome. Outcome is determined by what happens after entry — regime, timing, and the SL mechanics.

Finding 4 — Actionable fixes for the "SL then target" problem

Fix A — Widen the breakeven trigger (highest impact)

Current Stage 0 trigger0.4R (price moves 40% of risk in your direction)
Suggested trigger0.6R or 0.75R
EffectSL only moves to BE after a more confirmed move — fewer noise stops
Where to changebreakeven_ratio in engine.py (default 0.4)

Raising from 0.4R to 0.6R means the trade needs to confirm 60% of the risk distance before SL moves. Backtest both values to measure WR and PF delta.

Fix B — Widen the BE level itself

Current BE SLentry + 0.10R (just above entry)
Suggested BE SLentry + 0R (flat breakeven)
EffectMore room for the trade to breathe after Stage 0 triggers
Where to changeentry_premium + risk_distance * 0.10 → change 0.10 to 0

Currently when Stage 0 fires, SL moves to entry + 10% of risk = slightly above entry. Moving it to exactly entry (0R) gives the trade a few more points of breathing room before being stopped.
